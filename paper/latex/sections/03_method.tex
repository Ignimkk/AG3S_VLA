\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
In this section, we present \sys, which turns the task-relevant information inside a frozen VLA into explicit 3D collision and contact constraints. After formulating the problem (Sec.~\ref{sec:overview}), we describe the two core components, attention-guided 3D segmentation (Sec.~\ref{sec:grounding}) and a spatiotemporal 3D object memory (Sec.~\ref{sec:memory}), followed by the stage- and link-specific constraints they define (Sec.~\ref{sec:contact}) and the refinement of the action chunk under them (Sec.~\ref{sec:refine}).

\subsection{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:overview}
Given a language instruction $l$, RGB observations $I_t$, and the robot state $q_t$ at request $t$, a VLA policy $\pi_\theta$ predicts an action chunk
\begin{equation}
 A_t=[a_t,\dots,a_{t+H-1}]=\pi_\theta(I_t,q_t,l),
 \label{eq:policy}
\end{equation}
where $H$ is the chunk length. Since $\pi_\theta$ is trained to reproduce demonstrations, $A_t$ carries no guarantee that the robot clears the scene, and in clutter the constraint it must satisfy is itself task-dependent. For a collision sphere $i$ of the robot or of a grasped object, with centre $c_i(q)$, radius $r_i$, and margin $\mu_i$, the clearance at waypoint $k$ reads
\begin{equation}
 h_{ik}(q_k)=\delta_{i,t}\big(c_i(q_k)\big)-r_i-\mu_i\geq0 ,
 \label{eq:clearance}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_{i,t}$ is the signed distance field that sphere $i$ queries. A single field computed from all observed surfaces forbids the grasp itself, because the fingers must touch the target. The field therefore depends on three quantities that $\pi_\theta$ never outputs explicitly: the observed surface $\mathcal T_t$ of the target object, the manipulation stage $s_t$, and whether sphere $i$ belongs to a contact link in $\mathcal L$, the palm and fingers of the manipulating hand. Prior safety layers take the target region from an external segmenter or a primitive such as an ellipsoid~\cite{hu2026vlsa,park2026knows}, and the latter also frees the neighbor that touches the target.

\sys\ instead derives $\mathcal T_t$ from the policy's own forward pass, using in addition only calibrated depth and a robot model covered by collision spheres. Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows the components and Alg.~\ref{alg:request} the order in which they run at each request: the attention of the target noun is grown along depth into a candidate surface (\emph{target segmentation}, Sec.~\ref{sec:grounding}), a 3D object memory decides whether it is the target (Sec.~\ref{sec:memory}), the stage selects which surfaces each link may enter (Sec.~\ref{sec:contact}), and the chunk is refined under Eq.~\eqref{eq:clearance} (Sec.~\ref{sec:refine}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\sys\ at request $t$}
\label{alg:request}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $I_t$, depth, $q_t$, $l$, memory $\mathcal M$, grasp state $z$, last target $\bar{\mathcal T}$
\State $A_t,a\gets\pi_\theta(I_t,q_t,l)$ \Comment{chunk, noun attention}
\State $\mathcal G\gets$ surface graph of robot-masked depth
\State $m^*\gets(\Lambda+L_{\mathcal G})^{-1}\Lambda y$, seeds of Eq.~\eqref{eq:mass}
\State $R_t\gets$ region of $m^*$ with most seed mass
\State update candidates and attention lead in $\mathcal M$
\If{$z\in\{\textsc{closing},\textsc{held}\}$}\label{alg:l:assocbegin}
  \State $\mathcal T_t\gets$ pre-closure surface, moved with it
\ElsIf{$R_t$ overlaps the target in $\mathcal M$}
  \State $\mathcal T_t\gets R_t$
\Else
  \State $\mathcal T_t\gets\bar{\mathcal T}$ \Comment{abstain}
\EndIf\label{alg:l:assocend}
\State $z\gets$ grasp state from finger opening
\State $s_t\gets$ stage from $z$ and palm--target distance
\State $d_t,\ d_t^{\setminus\mathcal X}\gets$ ESDFs of the TSDF, Eq.~\eqref{eq:region}
\State attach $\mathcal T_t$ as spheres if $z=\textsc{held}$
\State $Q^*\gets$ SQP on Eq.~\eqref{eq:qp} with $\delta_{i,t}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:linkquery}
\State execute $Q^*$ if all $h_{ik}\geq0$, else \textsc{hold}\label{alg:l:check}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Attention-guided 3D segmentation}
\label{sec:grounding}
As discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}, 2D regions of interest cannot separate touching or visually similar objects. VLA attention indicates \emph{which} object the instruction refers to, but only at the resolution of image patches, whereas depth reveals \emph{where} surfaces end but not which of them is the target. To this end, we lift the observations into a unified 3D representation and jointly exploit point-wise attention scores and geometric continuity (target segmentation in Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}).

\paragraph{Unified 3D representation.}
The depth pixels of all cameras are lifted into the robot frame and voxelized into surface nodes with centroids $x_i$ and normals $n_i$; pixels on the robot's collision spheres are discarded, and a RANSAC support plane is marked as support. Each node keeps the image patches its pixels come from, so attention transfers to 3D without re-projection.

\paragraph{Point-wise attention scores.}
Let $a_p$ be the attention from the instruction's target-noun token to image patch $p$ in one fixed head of the frozen VLA\textcolor{red}{, selected once on development episodes}. Copying each patch score to every point under it would let large, distant surfaces outweigh the small object the noun refers to. We instead keep the top-ranked patches $\mathcal P$ and distribute the normalized score of each patch over the nodes it observes in proportion to pixel share,
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_i^+=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}\frac{N_{ip}}{N_p}\,
 \frac{a_p}{\max_{r\in\mathcal P}a_r},
 \label{eq:mass}
\end{equation}
where $N_p$ counts the retained pixels of patch $p$ and $N_{ip}$ those on node $i$. Each patch thus contributes one unit of evidence regardless of the number of points it covers.

\paragraph{Geometric continuity.}
A radius graph would join two touching objects. We connect two nodes only when neighboring pixels of the same image link them without a depth discontinuity, so that proximity alone cannot bridge a visible depth break, and weight each admitted edge by
\begin{equation}
 w_{ij}=\exp\!\left(-\frac{\|x_i-x_j\|^2}{\sigma_x^2}
 -\frac{1-|n_i^\top n_j|}{\sigma_n}\right).
 \label{eq:affinity}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Attention-guided segmentation.}
Let $\lambda_i^-=1$ on support nodes, $\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda^++\lambda^-)$, and $y_i=\lambda_i^+/\Lambda_{ii}$. With the Laplacian $L_{\mathcal G}$ of the surface graph $\mathcal G$, the target membership $m^*\in[0,1]^{|\mathcal G|}$ is
\begin{equation}
 m^*=\arg\min_{m}\ (m-y)^\top\Lambda(m-y)+m^\top L_{\mathcal G}\,m ,
 \label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
obtained from $(\Lambda+L_{\mathcal G})m^*=\Lambda y$ on the GPU. Attention decides which surface receives evidence, and geometric continuity decides how far it flows; components without a seed stay unknown, never free. Thresholding $m^*$ and keeping the connected region with the greatest seed mass yields the candidate surface $R_t$. The target surface thus comes from the policy's own attention and the scene geometry alone, without an external or separately trained segmentation network.

\subsection{Spatiotemporal 3D object memory}
\label{sec:memory}
The segmentation fixes the extent of a surface at one request, but not which object is the target across requests: attention can move to a similar object, and the approaching hand occludes and pushes the target exactly when contact is imminent. We therefore keep the target in a memory of 3D objects that integrates their spatial relationships with their history over time.

\paragraph{Spatial relationships.}
The lifted attention is grouped into 3D-connected candidates above the support. Candidates floating above it or forming the destination, such as an obstacle bar or the basket, are filtered out, and one that merges two touching objects is split by its attention share.

\paragraph{Temporal association.}
A candidate becomes, or replaces, the target only after leading the attention for several consecutive requests, and $R_t$ is accepted only if it overlaps that target (Alg.~\ref{alg:request}, lines~\ref{alg:l:assocbegin}--\ref{alg:l:assocend}). An occluded target keeps its last observed geometry, and a grasped one keeps its pre-closure geometry while the hand pushes it. The memory thereby separates \emph{identity}, decided over time, from \emph{extent}, decided at each request, and missing evidence can only keep or withdraw an accepted surface, never extend it.

\subsection{Task-conditioned collision constraints}
\label{sec:contact}
\label{sec:exemption}
\label{sec:phase}
Contact with the target must be permitted while grasping, restricted in the other stages (Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}), and granted only to the links meant to touch it. We now build $\delta_{i,t}$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:clearance} accordingly.

\paragraph{Task stage recognition.}
Since appearance cannot tell a gripper holding the object from one closed on nothing, a grasp state $z_t$ becomes \textsc{held} only when the measured finger opening stalls above the commanded width, and \textsc{placed} when the gripper reopens or the object slips out. With the palm--target distance, $z_t$ yields the stages of Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} from approach to retreat without any learned component beyond the frozen VLA; the thresholds are given in the supplementary material.

\paragraph{Target-removed map.}
The scene map $d_t$ is a Euclidean signed distance field (ESDF) computed with cuRobo~\cite{sundaralingam2026curobov2} from a TSDF of the robot-masked depth, coarse over the workspace and fine around the hand. For the contact links, we compute a target-removed map $d_t^{\setminus\mathcal X}$ from the same TSDF without erasing any voxel, by dropping the surface voxels $\mathcal X_t$ owned by the target from the seeds of the distance transform,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal X_t=\big\{x:\ d_T(x)<d_N(x)\big\}\ \cup\ \big\{x:\ d_T(x)\leq\epsilon_c\big\}\ \cup\ \mathcal H_t^{\omega},
 \label{eq:region}
\end{equation}
where $d_T$ and $d_N$ are the distances to the nearest sample of $\mathcal T_t$ and of any other observed surface. The first set keeps every seed closer to another surface, so the touching neighbor still seeds the map. \textcolor{red}{A contact allowance $\epsilon_c$ around the target}, together with the convex hull $\mathcal H_t^{\omega}$ of $\mathcal T_t$ applied just before the grasp, covers the unobserved back and interior of the target. Seeds below the target's bottom are kept to protect the support.

\paragraph{Link- and stage-specific queries.}
Each sphere queries
\begin{equation}
 \delta_{i,t}=
 \begin{cases}
 d_t^{\setminus\mathcal X}, & i\in\mathcal L\ \land\ s_t\in\{\textit{approach},\textit{grasp}\},\\
 d_t, & \text{otherwise},
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:linkquery}
\end{equation}
so only the fingers and palm of the manipulating hand may reach the target. \textcolor{red}{The clearance of Eq.~\eqref{eq:clearance} is imposed on the spheres of both palms and fingers; the forearms, the torso, and self-collision are not constrained.} Once $z_t=\textsc{held}$, the target joins the robot as attached spheres that query the environment, and its surface stops seeding the scene map; after release it is an obstacle again. Therefore, one observed scene yields different constraints for different links and stages, which a single collision map cannot express.

\subsection{Constrained action refinement}
\label{sec:refine}
Finally, trajectory optimization refines the chunk under these constraints while preserving its manipulation intent. With the reference waypoints $Q^{\mathrm{ref}}$ of $A_t$ and the deflection $D=Q-Q^{\mathrm{ref}}$, we solve
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 \min_{Q,\,\xi\geq0}\ &\|W_t D\|_F^2+w_{\mathrm s}\|\Delta^2Q\|_F^2+w_\xi\textstyle\sum_{i,k}\xi_{ik}+\mathbb 1_t^{\mathrm{g}}\,w_{\mathrm d}\|\Delta D\|_F^2\\
 \text{s.t.}\ \ &h_{ik}(q_k)\geq-\xi_{ik}\ \ \forall i,k,\qquad Q\in\mathcal Q ,
\end{split}
 \label{eq:qp}
\end{equation}
by sequential quadratic programming with OSQP~\cite{stellato2020osqp}, where $W_t$ is a diagonal joint weighting\textcolor{red}{, lower for the wrist joints near the target}, $\mathcal Q$ holds joint and motion limits, and $w_\xi$ dominates. The deflection-rate term acts only after the grasp ($\mathbb 1_t^{\mathrm g}$), keeping an avoidance deflection from snapping back at chunk boundaries without pulling the hand off the grasp pose before it. \textcolor{red}{A continuity term to the previously executed chunk is gated in the same way.} A failed safety check on the nonlinear clearance makes the robot \textsc{hold} (Alg.~\ref{alg:request}, line~\ref{alg:l:check}), which we count as an intervention, not a success.
