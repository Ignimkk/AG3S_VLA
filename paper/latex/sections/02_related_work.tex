\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Vision-Language-Action Models.}
Recent advances in VLMs have facilitated the development of VLA models that integrate visual perception, language understanding, and robotic action generation. Early approaches, such as RT-2~\cite{zitkovich2023rt2} and OpenVLA~\cite{kim2024openvla}, formulate robotic control as an autoregressive sequence prediction problem by discretizing continuous actions into tokens. More recent models, including $\pi_0$~\cite{black2024pi0} and $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{intelligence2025pi05}, employ flow-matching-based action generation and large-scale multimodal training to improve manipulation performance and generalization across diverse tasks and environments. To further enhance spatial understanding, SpatialVLA~\cite{qu2025spatialvla} incorporates 3D positional representations, while PointVLA~\cite{li2025pointvla} integrates point cloud features into pretrained VLA policies. Despite these advances, improved spatial representations and action generation do not inherently ensure that predicted actions satisfy explicit geometric collision constraints, particularly in cluttered environments involving close interactions between target objects and surrounding obstacles.

\paragraph{Collision-Aware and Constraint-Guided Manipulation.}
Several studies have integrated safety mechanisms into VLA policies to mitigate unintended collisions during robotic manipulation. SafeVLA~\cite{zhang2025safevla} incorporates safety requirements through constrained reinforcement learning, while AEGIS~\cite{hu2026vlsa} employs vision-language-guided obstacle identification and 3D geometric representations to enforce collision avoidance using control barrier functions. KNOWS~\cite{park2026knows} instead identifies the manipulation target from the attention of a frozen VLA, while obtaining object geometry from an external segmentation model and approximating each object with an ellipsoid. Other approaches, such as neuro-symbolic safety guidance~\cite{english2026constrainedfm}, incorporate collision constraints into flow-matching-based action generation, enabling predictive trajectory correction. VLA-Stem~\cite{zou2026vlastem} further introduces a lightweight safety policy that adapts VLA-generated motion references to spatial constraints and dynamic obstacles. These methods demonstrate the effectiveness of safety-aware action generation and correction, highlighting the importance of reliable geometric representations for enforcing collision constraints. Frozen VLA representations have also been shown to encode task progress and failure signals~\cite{bhardwaj2026progress,gu2025safe}, suggesting that policy-internal features carry task-relevant information beyond action prediction.

In parallel, several studies have investigated language-grounded geometric representations for robotic manipulation. VoxPoser~\cite{huang2023voxposer} constructs 3D value maps from language instructions and visual observations to guide model-based motion planning. ReKep~\cite{huang2024rekep} represents manipulation tasks through relational constraints over 3D keypoints, enabling optimization-based action generation. Other works, such as LMAffordance3D~\cite{zhu2025lmaffordance3d}, ground language-specified interaction regions on 3D object point clouds. Although these approaches provide spatial guidance for robot planning, constructing collision constraints for manipulation additionally requires distinguishing the intended target from nearby objects and determining which contacts are permissible throughout task execution. Motivated by this distinction and unlike KNOWS, which uses attention only to select among externally segmented objects, our work leverages internal VLA attention and hidden-state features together with multi-view RGB-D observations to identify task-relevant 3D surfaces and construct geometric collision constraints with task-dependent contact permissions. These constraints are designed to support downstream trajectory optimization while preserving the manipulation objective.
