\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\paragraph{Vision-language-action policies and grounding.}
VLA policies map images, language, and robot state to actions, from tokenized policies~\cite{brohan2023rt1,zitkovich2023rt2,kim2024openvla} to generalist~\cite{octo2024} and flow-matching action experts~\cite{black2024pi0,intelligence2025pi05}. Several works expose object structure to the policy: RoboGround supplies grounding masks as an intermediate representation~\cite{huang2025roboground}, OBEYED-VLA separates object-centric perception from action reasoning~\cite{vo2026obeyed}, and GuidedVLA supervises action attention toward task-relevant regions~\cite{jia2026guidedvla}. All of them change the policy's input or training. \sys\ instead keeps the VLA frozen and reads the structure it already has, turning it into an external constraint rather than a better input.

\paragraph{Attention, graphs, and target extent.}
Self-attention in vision transformers concentrates on objects~\cite{caron2021dino}, and a few heads of a frozen vision-language model localize referred objects~\cite{kang2025fewheads}. KNOWS already uses internal VLA attention to pick the target for a safety filter and combines it with externally segmented geometry~\cite{park2026knows}, so reusing attention is not by itself our distinction. Grouping on graphs is equally established, from normalized cuts~\cite{shi2000ncut} and graph-based segmentation~\cite{felzenszwalb2004graph} to seeded random walks and label propagation~\cite{grady2006randomwalker,zhou2004consistency}; TokenCut applies normalized cuts to transformer features~\cite{wang2022tokencut}. Our graph encodes observed depth adjacency rather than feature similarity, internal features act on region identity across observations rather than on the boundary, and the contribution lies in converting coarse policy evidence into an observed 3D extent and that extent into a restricted contact exception.

\paragraph{Geometric constraints on learned motion.}
Collision avoidance traditionally acts on distance fields through potential fields~\cite{khatib1986potential}, trajectory optimization~\cite{ratliff2009chomp,schulman2014trajopt}, or control barrier functions~\cite{ames2019cbf}, with fields fused from depth~\cite{curless1996volumetric,oleynikova2017voxblox,millane2024nvblox} and optimized on the GPU by cuRobo~\cite{sundaralingam2023curobo,sundaralingam2026curobov2}. Language models can compose 3D value maps or keypoint constraints for a planner~\cite{huang2023voxposer,huang2024rekep}. For VLA policies, MoManipVLA refines motion by geometric optimization~\cite{wu2025momanipvla}, VLSA and KNOWS filter actions with barrier constraints~\cite{hu2026vlsa,park2026knows}, AFI intervenes with spatial affordance fields~\cite{xu2026afi}, and per-link filtering has been proposed for moving hazards~\cite{agarwal2026multilink}; generative policies can also be steered at sampling time by guidance fields, constrained flow matching, reactive avoidance, or differentiable optimization~\cite{song2026omniguide,english2026constrainedfm,guo2026nudge,xu2024leto,li2025lano3dp,zhan2026seam}. Allowed collisions between specific links and attached objects are standard in planning frameworks~\cite{coleman2014moveit}, and AutoMoMa switches an object's collision treatment between approach and manipulation, with known scene geometry and offline~\cite{niu2026automoma}. We build on these primitives rather than claiming new ones. What \sys\ adds is the representation the refiner consumes: a policy-grounded observed surface that is excluded for designated contact links while remaining an obstacle for every other link, and that changes role with the task.

\paragraph{Task progress and interaction state.}
Frozen VLA representations carry information about task progress, success, and failure~\cite{bhardwaj2026progress,zhang2026frozenvlas,gu2025safe,zhang2026probeact,jeong2026pathheads}. \sys\ uses a sub-task probe to time its contact rules and augments it with gripper state and target--hand geometry; its consistency alert is a failure signal of the same family, read from the grounding rather than from the action. We distinguish this estimate of \emph{intent} from physical \emph{attachment}: a predicted placement phase does not establish a grasp, and in our representation that difference decides whether the target is an environmental obstacle or geometry carried by the robot. Safety can also be trained into the policy~\cite{zhang2025safevla}; we leave the policy unchanged. Benchmarks such as SafeLIBERO~\cite{hu2026vlsa}, built on LIBERO~\cite{liu2023libero}, LIBERO-Safety~\cite{cui2026liberosafety}, and SafeVLA-Bench~\cite{fan2026safevlabench} measure the resulting success--safety trade-off; we report on SafeLIBERO in Sec.~\ref{sec:safelibero}.
