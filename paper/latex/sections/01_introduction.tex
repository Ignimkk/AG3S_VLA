\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Robotic manipulation tasks, such as pick-and-place operations in manufacturing and logistics, require robots to identify target objects from visual observations and execute appropriate actions according to task instructions. Recent advances in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have significantly improved visual understanding and language-grounded reasoning by learning semantic relationships between visual observations and natural language. However, semantic understanding alone is insufficient for generating continuous robot actions. To bridge this gap, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models integrate pretrained vision-language representations with robotic demonstrations to learn policies that directly predict robot actions from observations and task instructions. Representative models, including RT-2~\cite{zitkovich2023rt2}, OpenVLA~\cite{kim2024openvla}, and $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{intelligence2025pi05}, have demonstrated language-conditioned manipulation capabilities across diverse objects and tasks, as well as promising generalization to unseen environments.

The fundamental capability of VLA models lies in connecting visual observations and language instructions to robot action spaces. Typically, VLA models are trained to predict individual actions or continuous action chunks conditioned on visual observations, robot states, and task instructions. Through this process, these models can implicitly learn not only semantic object information but also spatial relationships and manipulation patterns relevant to task execution. Consequently, VLA models can generate diverse manipulation behaviors without requiring explicitly predefined motion rules or trajectories for each task. \textbf{However, the ability to predict actions is fundamentally distinct from the ability to explicitly represent the geometric and physical constraints that those actions must satisfy.} Since conventional VLA policies primarily learn to generate actions consistent with demonstration data, their training objectives neither require an explicit representation of object-level 3D occupancy nor guarantee that the generated actions satisfy robot--environment collision constraints or task-dependent contact conditions.

These limitations become particularly critical in cluttered environments where multiple objects are closely arranged or target objects are partially occluded. For instance, picking an apple adjacent to an obstacle requires the robot not only to identify the intended target but also to distinguish its 3D surface from surrounding objects and determine which regions permit contact and which must be avoided. When multiple visually similar objects are present, 2D regions of interest alone may be insufficient to reliably determine object identity and geometric boundaries. Furthermore, contact with the target object must be permitted during grasping, while the same contact may need to be restricted during other manipulation stages. Therefore, safe manipulation requires both accurate object-level spatial representations and task-dependent contact conditions. \textbf{However, task-relevant information encoded in VLA attention maps and hidden states is not directly available as explicit 3D collision constraints.}

Recent studies have explored attention-based target identification~\cite{park2026knows}, obstacle estimation using external perception models~\cite{hu2026vlsa}, and constraint-based action refinement~\cite{hu2026vlsa,park2026knows} to improve the safety of VLA policies. However, some existing approaches rely on additional segmentation networks to distinguish object regions or approximate complex object geometries using simplified spatial representations~\cite{hu2026vlsa,park2026knows}. Moreover, even when target and non-target objects can be distinguished, maintaining consistent target identity and geometric boundaries remains challenging in the presence of occlusion and visually similar objects. These challenges motivate a method that directly exploits task-relevant information embedded in VLA representations to segment target object surfaces and transform observed 3D geometry into explicit collision and contact constraints tailored to different manipulation stages.

To address these challenges, we propose \textbf{\sys}, an Attention-Guided 3D Scene Segmentation framework for generating task-conditioned collision constraints from VLA internal representations. \sys\ identifies task-relevant objects using VLA attention maps and hidden-state features without relying on external segmentation models. Specifically, multi-view RGB-D observations are lifted into a unified 3D spatial representation, where point-wise attention scores and geometric continuity are jointly exploited to segment observed object surfaces. Furthermore, we introduce a spatiotemporal 3D object memory that integrates spatial relationships and temporal feature information to distinguish visually similar objects and preserve target identity under partial occlusion. Based on these representations, \sys\ constructs 3D geometric descriptions of target and non-target objects and defines collision and contact constraints according to manipulation stages and robot links. The resulting constraints are subsequently incorporated into trajectory optimization to refine VLA-generated action chunks while preserving the intended manipulation objective. In this way, \sys\ bridges the gap between implicit task-relevant representations learned by VLA policies and explicit geometric constraints required for safe robotic manipulation.

We evaluate \sys\ on the SafeLIBERO~\cite{hu2026vlsa} and LIBERO-Safety~\cite{cui2026liberosafety} benchmarks, as well as real-world robotic manipulation tasks. \textcolor{red}{Extensive experiments demonstrate that \sys\ outperforms existing VLA-based methods in manipulation success and safety performance, achieving state-of-the-art results. Real-world evaluations further show strong generalization in cluttered environments involving visually similar and partially occluded objects.} These results highlight the effectiveness of leveraging VLA internal representations for 3D spatial perception and explicit collision constraint generation.

\textbf{Our contributions are threefold:}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A Novel \sys\ Framework:} We propose \sys, a 3D-aware framework that leverages VLA internal representations to generate task-conditioned collision constraints for robotic manipulation. \textcolor{red}{To the best of our knowledge, \sys\ is the first VLA framework to perform task-conditioned 3D object surface segmentation directly from policy-internal representations without relying on external or separately trained segmentation networks.}
\item \textbf{Attention-Guided 3D Segmentation and Object Memory:} We introduce a 3D perception module that segments object surfaces by combining VLA attention scores with geometric continuity. By leveraging hidden-state features and 3D spatial relationships, our method distinguishes visually similar objects and maintains target identity and location under partial occlusion through a spatiotemporal 3D object memory.
\item \textbf{\textcolor{red}{State-of-the-Art Performance and Real-World Generalization:}} \textcolor{red}{Extensive evaluations on SafeLIBERO and LIBERO-Safety demonstrate that \sys\ outperforms existing VLA-based methods in manipulation success and safety performance. Real-world experiments further confirm strong generalization and high success rates in cluttered and occluded environments,} validating the effectiveness of VLA-internal 3D perception for task-conditioned constraint generation.
\end{itemize}
