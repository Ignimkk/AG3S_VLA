Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models map observations and language instructions directly to robot actions, with no explicit representation of the 3D occupancy of objects and no guarantee that the actions satisfy collision and contact constraints. Existing safety layers take the shape of the obstacle or the target from an external detector or segmenter, even when the VLA's attention selects the target, and commonly enclose it in an ellipsoid. In clutter, this frees the neighbor touching the target or blocks the grasp, and no existing layer specifies which link may touch the target and when. We present AG3S-VLA, an Attention-Guided 3D Scene Segmentation framework that grounds the target's surface and builds task-conditioned collision constraints for selective contact from a frozen VLA's internal representations and the gripper's feedback. The attention of the action queries, lifted into 3D and propagated along depth-continuous surfaces, segments the target's observed surface. A spatiotemporal 3D object memory keeps the target's identity under occlusion and among similar objects. The policy's hidden state, checked against the gripper's grasp state, gives the manipulation stage. While grasping, only the contact links may enter the target, the grasped object then joins the robot, and every other observed surface remains an obstacle. A trajectory optimizer refines the VLA's action chunk under these constraints, preserving its intent without updating the VLA. On SafeLIBERO and LIBERO-Safety with the same frozen π0.5, AG3S-VLA substantially improves collision avoidance and safe success over existing safety layers with task success on par, and transfers to a real robot.
